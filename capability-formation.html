Capability Formation

Develop the ability to perform, retain knowledge and improve.

Capability Formation includes people, knowledge, organisations and industrial systems. We help define the performance required, examine what enables it, and shape a practical pathway for learning, technology absorption and sustained improvement.

  1. 01Perform the work
  2. 02Establish acceptance
  3. 03Retain the ability
  4. 04Learn & improve

Four connected questions, revisited as work, people and technology change.

Reduced motion is enabled. The questions remain visible.

Begin with what must become possible.

Choose the capability need closest to your work. Explore what people and organisations must become able to do.

People and productive roles

A team needs to perform particular work reliably, with relevant knowledge, practice, supervision and feedback.

A useful starting questionWhat work must the team perform reliably, and under which conditions?

Organisations and knowledge

Important know-how depends on individuals or temporary support, and needs to become part of how the organisation works.

A useful starting questionWhich knowledge must remain usable when individuals or external support change?

Technology absorption

A technology is available, but effective use requires local understanding, operating routines, maintenance and the ability to adapt and improve.

A useful starting questionWhat must local teams understand to operate, maintain, adapt and improve the technology?

Industrial and institutional systems

Performance depends on several organisations, shared technical functions and clear responsibilities across their interfaces.

A useful starting questionWhich organisational interfaces or shared functions determine whether the whole system performs?

Four questions guide the assessment.

Explore four assessment questions and the possible outputs they inform. They are connected questions, not a one-time checklist.

Can the work be performed?

Define the productive activity and examine evidence of actual performance under relevant conditions. Plans, assets and training attendance alone do not settle this question.

Possible outputs to discuss
  • Capability definition and evidence baseline
  • Role and dependency map
  • Formation pathway

Who accepts the performance?

Identify the customer, authority or qualified verifier, the applicable standard, and their ability to reject inadequate performance. Make the acceptance requirements explicit.

Possible outputs to discuss
  • Capability definition and evidence baseline
  • Performance and improvement framework

Will the ability remain?

Examine whether performance can be repeated through personnel changes and over time. Look at routines, records, relationships, training and continuity arrangements.

Possible outputs to discuss
  • Learning and knowledge-retention plan
  • Handover and support-transition plan

Can the system improve?

Examine measurement, feedback, problem-solving and the authority to change how work is done. Identify how learning can become better performance.

Possible outputs to discuss
  • Formation pathway
  • Performance and improvement framework

A pathway tied to performance and evidence.

The scope and deliverables are agreed for the engagement. They may include:

Capability definition and evidence baseline

The activity, operating conditions, acceptance requirements and available performance evidence, with gaps made visible.

Role and dependency map

The people, knowledge, routines, technical resources and organisational interfaces needed to perform the work.

Formation pathway

A sequence of practice, technical development, organisational changes and validation, with owners and decision points.

Learning and knowledge-retention plan

How expertise will be shared, recorded, taught and applied across teams, including continuity through personnel changes.

Handover and support-transition plan

What the organisation must demonstrate before temporary support changes or withdraws, and what continuing services remain necessary.

Performance and improvement framework

Agreed evidence, review routines, feedback and responsibilities for sustaining performance and addressing problems.

Build capability in the organisation that must use it.

Training, specialist advice and shared facilities can contribute to formation. The receiving organisation needs opportunities to practise, use judgment, retain knowledge and improve. Support arrangements should clarify what becomes internal and what remains a continuing external service.

A completed course, installed system or delivered report is an output. The capability question concerns accepted performance that can be repeated and improved. This assessment supports agreed decisions; it does not constitute a certification or guarantee of results.
