Find your starting point.

Explore the perspective that fits your work. Individual expertise, team capability and learning partnerships are connected but have different responsibilities.

Professionals and technical specialists

Connect your expertise with a clearly defined problem and a relevant contribution.

Questions to explore

  • What work can you demonstrate, and where could that experience contribute?
  • What role, collaboration or learning context are you seeking?

Possible next steps

  • A focused discussion of expertise, evidence and possible contribution
  • A clearer scope for a potential collaboration, where there is a relevant need
Organisations developing their teams

Start with the performance the organisation needs and the roles that must deliver it.

Questions to explore

  • What must the team become able to do, and against which standards?
  • What opportunities for practice, feedback and knowledge retention are missing?

Possible next steps

  • A role and capability-needs assessment
  • A proposed workplace-learning and knowledge-retention plan
Education, training and industry partners

Connect learning pathways with actual roles, working environments and organisational demand.

Questions to explore

  • Which roles and productive problems should shape the learning?
  • Who provides practice, supervision, assessment and a setting where learning can be used?

Possible next steps

  • A partnership and role-development brief
  • A proposed pathway connecting instruction, applied practice and evidence of performance

Build learning around the work.

  1. Define the role and expected performance. Specify the task, its operating context, the relevant standards and who will judge the result.
  2. Examine the starting point. Review existing experience and performance evidence. Identify gaps in knowledge, practice, tools, supervision or organisational support.
  3. Connect learning with application. Develop a pathway involving relevant instruction, supervised practice, feedback and opportunities to solve real problems.
  4. Retain and improve. Plan how knowledge is shared, recorded, taught and maintained through personnel changes, with feedback that supports continued improvement.

Clear responsibilities for development.

The commissioning organisation, employer, learner, supervisor and assessor may be different parties. The engagement should define their responsibilities, access to practice, evidence requirements and Istawra’s agreed contribution.

Tools support the work; people own the judgment.

Istawra’s private advisory tools are intended to support analysis and the development of practical proposals. Expert contribution, evidence review and responsibility for decisions remain explicit within each engagement.

These are areas for discussion, not an enrolment catalogue or a promise of employment, placement or certification. Specific opportunities and learning arrangements require an agreed scope.
