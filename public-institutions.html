Public Institutions

Build capabilities that endure and accumulate.

Connect national ambition with the productive capabilities, institutional responsibilities and learning needed to sustain progress.

We help public institutions bring strategy, investment, research, industry and education into a coherent programme of work. The starting question is what people, organisations and industrial systems must become able to do—and how that ability can remain and grow over time.

What are you working to strengthen?

Start with the capability to be formed, the demand it will serve and the institutions needed to sustain it. Then examine dependencies, resources and the next decision.

Strategy and institutional coherence

When priorities, budgets and institutional responsibilities need to support the same productive outcome.

  • Map dependencies between strategy, funding and delivery
  • Clarify decision ownership and institutional interfaces
  • Examine what to protect, redesign, defer or stop under constraints

Investment and ecosystem development

When attracting an innovative enterprise also requires demand, research, skills, suppliers and a credible route to local operations.

  • Connect capability gaps with suitable enterprises and partners
  • Define handovers from attraction to establishment and ongoing support
  • Assess commercial logic, absorption capacity and shared industrial needs

Research and capability accumulation

When research and programmes need a clearer route into repeatable productive performance.

  • Connect technical problems with research, testing and validation
  • Design bounded learning assignments before wider commitments
  • Define the knowledge, routines and capabilities that should remain and be reused

A practical starting point.

The scope and deliverables are agreed for each engagement. A focused initial assignment may take the following shape.

  1. Define the capability and the decision

    Agree the productive outcome, who needs it, who will accept its performance and who owns the decision. Identify the commissioning client, beneficiaries and delivery owners separately.

    Possible output
    A decision brief defining the capability sought, acceptance criteria, scope and accountable owners.

  2. Map the system and test the response

    Examine demand, people, technology, suppliers, research, regulation and resources together. Compare options and identify the dependencies that a smaller scope must still preserve.

    Possible output
    An options and dependency map showing evidence gaps, resource needs and sequencing.

  3. Specify what should remain

    Define evidence of performance, knowledge retention, learning and reuse. Set out responsibilities, sequencing, handover and the separate decisions required before further implementation.

    Possible output
    A capability and handover plan specifying performance evidence, retained knowledge and responsibilities.

Separate roles. Shared understanding.

Commissioning an assignment, making a decision, benefiting from the outcome and delivering the work are different responsibilities. We make those distinctions explicit.

Commissioning client
Sets the brief, authorises the assignment and agrees its scope and acceptance criteria.
Decision owner
Holds the authority to choose a course of action and approve the relevant commitments.
Beneficiaries
The people, organisations or systems whose needs and intended outcomes inform the work.
Delivery owners
The client teams, partners or other appointed parties responsible for implementation and ongoing operations.
Istawra
Provides the analysis, design, facilitation, capability formation or delivery support agreed in the scope. Decision authority remains with the designated owner.

Evidence and capability at the centre.

Make the basis for decisions visible

We distinguish source-supported facts, assumptions, proposed options and owner decisions. Important uncertainties and evidence gaps remain visible as the work progresses.

Build beyond the assignment

Capability formation includes knowledge transfer, organisational learning and stronger industrial systems. It helps institutions develop the ability to sustain and improve the work.

Connect alignment with feasibility

National alignment informs opportunity assessment and risk mitigation. Commercial viability, returns and government support require their own evidence and decisions; none follows automatically from alignment.

Respect institutional boundaries

The engagement defines access, confidentiality and attribution. Independent identities and authorship are preserved, and confidential work stays within its agreed boundaries.
