The laptop is the main line, and the facility is where it ends up.

A visual account of the design as amended on 13 September 2026: what changes in the order of work, how one hearing actually moves, why the seats are modelled for fidelity rather than for outcomes, and what the tool proposed to the Center of Government would and would not do.

Istawra Consulting for the KAPSARC School of Public Policy · amends Policy Design Facility, design v1

One · What moves

The facility stops being a gate and becomes a deployment.

The design document put a room at KAPSARC on the path to every session. The amendment proves the method on a laptop, with KAPSARC developing the seats alongside the seats' author, and takes a record to the Center of Government. The facility, if it is built at all, comes after the Center of Government, as one place the national tool runs.

Two · How one hearing moves

The vault shapes what a seat is told. The engine alone writes what happened.

This is the loop as it runs on the laptop today. Nothing a model says becomes state except by passing the guards and reaching the engine; and nothing the engine records reaches the vault except as a copy for the seat to read next time. The seats' author sits in two places at once: in the chair, and at the vault with a pen.

Three · The first amendment

Seats are modelled for fidelity to their institution, not for the outcome we want.

Every session is meant to yield a policy hypothesis worth carrying to the Center of Government. That is only true if the room argued. The difference between a room that argues and a room that agrees is not visible in the outcome; it is visible in the arrows.

Principle one. A seat is an institution, not a mouthpiece.

Playbooks are extracted from the written strategies to capture how each institution really argues: its mandate, its constraint, what it protects, what would move it. A claim of institutional fidelity requires validation by the institution concerned. The finance seat that resists well has learned. The one that concedes has been flattered.

Four · The second amendment

In the national tool, the AI has no substantive decision authority. It is the memory and the challenger.

"Real people empowered by a dedicated AI that learned from the simulations" is the right sentence if the AI's job is drawn tightly. What it learned is the seat files: how each seat has argued, what challenges it drew, which errors recur; an institutional-perspective memory until the institution concerned validates its seat. What it does with that is bounded.

Principle two. The AI's reach ends at the register.

It holds the memory, plays the challenger and the evidence seat, fills an empty chair under a label naming occupant and origin, and refuses invented figures from anyone. Rules enforce; AI argues and challenges; people decide. A real recommendation still requires validation outside the room, and the tool says so on its own screen.

Five · The order of work

Six steps, each gated on the one before, ending at the Center of Government.

The order is the information here: a powerful Mudawalat is one that finishes a session, so the arc comes before the seats, the seats before a second case, the register before anyone is asked to trust it.

  1. 1

    Finish the arc

    Decisions three to six, the period clock, the branch, the debrief; the five sentences and the hypothesis exported. Today two decisions of six are mapped in the room; the rest exist in the engine as period actions.

    Gate: one whole session on the laptop, with a pinned, replayable record.

  2. 2

    Playbooks for every seat, the chair and the moderator

    Extracted from the written strategies for fidelity; heard across whole sessions.

    Gate: seat-fidelity review passed; each seat's version approved for use.

  3. 3

    A second case to the same template

    Critical minerals, or another sector, so the Center of Government sees a method and not a drone exercise. An independent balance read of both cases.

    Gate: both cases pass the narrative, evidentiary and transition balance tests.

  4. 4

    The register, and its governance

    Hypotheses across sessions, anonymised, with a rule for how one graduates to a research question. Until an institution validates its seat, the seat files are an institutional-perspective memory, not evidence of how a ministry argues; they are sensitive nonetheless, and KAPSARC decides who holds them before anything is proposed.

    Gate: governance written and agreed at KAPSARC.

  5. 5

    A mixed room on a local network

    Real KAPSARC people in some seats and models in the rest, so participant learning is seen rather than asserted. A small piece of the facility design, not the facility.

    Gate: one cohort session with a debrief at all three levels.

  6. 6

    The proposal to the Center of Government

    A national tool, with a record behind it, bounded as in figure four. The facility follows as one place it runs.

    Gate: the Center of Government decides.
