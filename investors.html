02

Investor starting position

Your starting position adds a different set of questions. It does not determine legal nationality or eligibility.

Saudi-based / national investor

Develop domestic opportunities, strengthen portfolio capabilities and assess suitable international partners.

  • What capabilities and opportunities can your existing Saudi presence support?
  • Where could technology, partners or shared facilities strengthen the investment case?

International / foreign investor

Examine Saudi market entry, local demand, operating requirements and the right partnership model.

  • What local customer demand and operating evidence support entry?
  • Which establishment, regulatory, partner and capability requirements need specialist review?

Both / cross-border structure

Clarify the roles of domestic and international capital, owners and operating entities.

  • Who invests, who owns and who operates in each jurisdiction?
  • What governance, partner contributions and local delivery arrangements need to be agreed?

Private equity: connect acquisition, development and the next owner.

Examine the business as part of a wider system of customers, technology, suppliers, management and capital. The investment thesis needs a practical route to delivery.

Before acquisition

What must be true for the investment thesis to work?

  • Examine demand, operating performance, management and supplier dependencies.
  • Identify capability gaps, integration needs and the evidence required from financial, legal and technical specialists.

During ownership

What will make the business stronger and more valuable?

  • Connect operational improvements with accountable owners, resources and measurable results.
  • Assess technology, partners and complementary acquisitions; plan how knowledge and capability will remain in the business.

Before the next ownership transition

What would a suitable next owner need to see?

  • Compare potential buyer types, strategic fit and evidence of sustainable performance.
  • Prepare management continuity, credible reporting and a clear account of remaining dependencies.

04

Where are you in the investment journey?

Choose a stage to explore the questions, evidence and next decision. You can move between stages in any order.

Shape a search thesis

Translate a concrete need into an actionable search for firms, technology and business models.

Questions to resolve

  • What customer problem or productive gap matters?
  • What maturity, business model and company profile could address it?

Evidence to examine

  • Demand and customer requirements
  • Existing portfolio fit, technology maturity and capability gaps

The next decisionAgree the search thesis and the criteria for considering candidates.

Find and compare opportunities

Look across existing investments and suitable external firms, including established innovative SMEs.

Questions to resolve

  • Which candidates fit the thesis and why?
  • What capabilities and complementary partners would each require?

Evidence to examine

  • Comparable candidate profiles and source quality
  • Commercial traction, technology evidence and local operating requirements

The next decisionSelect the candidates that warrant deeper examination and record unresolved gaps.

Examine the investment case

Keep commercial underwriting and investment authority explicit. Consider an exit pathway at entry.

Questions to resolve

  • What supports demand, economics and the financing case?
  • Which assumptions could change the decision, and what exit routes are plausible?

Evidence to examine

  • Commercial, technical and financial diligence
  • Downside assumptions, governance, financing needs and possible exit conditions

The next decisionPrepare the evidence and open questions for the investor’s own decision process.

Establish local proof

Connect the opportunity with a real customer, a responsible sponsor and a bounded validation plan.

Questions to resolve

  • Who needs the solution and can accept its performance?
  • What would a funded pilot need to demonstrate technically and commercially?

Evidence to examine

  • Customer requirements, sponsor ownership and a pilot brief
  • Validation criteria, operating dependencies, costs and evidence of willingness to pay

The next decisionDecide whether a defined pilot merits commitment, and set its acceptance criteria.

Develop and retain capability

Examine what can become established locally and what the business can sustain and improve.

Questions to resolve

  • What useful capability would remain if the external company left?
  • Which people, processes, suppliers and technical functions need to develop?

Evidence to examine

  • Capability responsibilities, partner contributions and learning arrangements
  • Repeatable performance, retained know-how and realistic routes to scale

The next decisionAgree a commercially grounded capability-development plan and responsibilities.

Prepare for exit and learning

Revisit the pathway considered at entry and capture what deployment has taught the investment team.

Questions to resolve

  • Which exit options remain feasible under current conditions?
  • What bottlenecks and learning should inform the next investment thesis?

Evidence to examine

  • Business performance, buyer or market requirements and dependencies
  • Lessons from customers, regulation, suppliers and capability development

The next decisionAssess the available options and update the investment thesis. An exit is never assured.

Two distinct assessments

The commercial case

Each opportunity needs its own assessment of demand, economics, risk, capital requirements and exit possibilities. Investment decisions remain with the investor and its authorised decision-makers.

An additional capability perspective

Where relevant, examine technology importance, dependency exposure, applicability in Saudi Arabia, capability retention and future options. This perspective informs judgment; it does not determine an investment decision or guarantee commercial viability, returns or government support.
