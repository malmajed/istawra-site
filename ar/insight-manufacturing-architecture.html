Chapter 1

Strategic Context: The Need for a Saudi Advanced Manufacturing Base

1.1 Global Industrial Competition and Manufacturing Sovereignty

Over the past three decades, advanced manufacturing has become the primary foundation of national economic power and technological sovereignty. Countries that control critical manufacturing capabilities are able to shape global supply chains, capture high-value industrial activity, and maintain resilience in times of geopolitical or economic disruption.

The COVID-19 pandemic, geopolitical tensions, and supply chain shocks have demonstrated that dependence on foreign manufacturing for strategic goods carries substantial risk. As a result, many countries are actively pursuing policies aimed at strengthening domestic manufacturing capacity and securing critical supply chains.

Advanced manufacturing ecosystems typically combine several essential elements:

• a strong anchor industry capable of generating large and consistent demand • deep supplier networks producing components and modules • process-capability platforms such as precision casting, advanced forming, robotics, and additive manufacturing • engineering, design, and quality infrastructure • workforce and technical training systems • coordinated financing and industrial policy support

Countries that succeed in building such ecosystems are able to sustain competitive industrial sectors across multiple domains including automotive, aerospace, medical devices, defense, and energy infrastructure.

1.2 Manufacturing as the Backbone of Economic Diversification

For Saudi Arabia, building an advanced manufacturing base is central to long-term economic diversification and technological capability development.

While the Kingdom possesses substantial strengths in energy, petrochemicals, and large-scale infrastructure, the industrial system remains relatively shallow in certain high-value manufacturing domains. Many complex products and critical components are still imported despite the existence of strong domestic demand.

Developing domestic manufacturing capabilities offers several national benefits:

3.2 Overview of Investment Casting Technology

Investment casting, also known as the lost-wax casting process, is a manufacturing technique used to produce complex metal components with high dimensional accuracy and excellent surface quality.

The process is widely used in industries that require precision components, including:

• aerospace • automotive • medical equipment • industrial machinery • energy systems

Compared with traditional casting techniques, investment casting offers several advantages:

• ability to produce complex geometries • near-net-shape manufacturing reducing machining requirements • high material utilization efficiency • excellent surface finish • high dimensional precision

These characteristics make investment casting particularly valuable for producing components that would otherwise require extensive machining or complex fabrication processes.

3.3 Core Manufacturing Processes in the Manufacturing Platform B Platform

The Manufacturing Platform B facility integrates several manufacturing stages that together form the complete investment casting workflow.

Slurry Preparation

The process begins with the preparation of ceramic slurry mixtures used to create the molds that will shape molten metal. High-quality slurry preparation ensures consistency in shell strength, thickness, and thermal performance.

Digital Pattern and Core Printing

Modern investment casting increasingly uses 3D printing technologies to produce patterns and cores. In the Manufacturing Platform B system, binder-jet printing and polymer pattern printing technologies are used to create precise models that will later form the cavities within the ceramic mold.

This digital approach offers several advantages:

• rapid prototyping • reduced tooling costs • ability to produce complex internal geometries • faster design iteration

Powder Handling and Recycling

During the printing process, unused powder materials must be recovered, cleaned, and reconditioned for reuse. Efficient powder management systems reduce material costs and improve production sustainability.

Pattern Post-Processing

Printed patterns and cores undergo post-processing steps such as cleaning, drying, and stabilization to ensure they maintain dimensional accuracy during later stages of production.

Ceramic Shell Building

One of the most critical steps in investment casting is the creation of the ceramic mold.

In the Manufacturing Platform B platform, robotic systems automate the shell-building process. Patterns are repeatedly dipped in ceramic slurry and coated with fine sand layers until a multi-layer shell is formed. Controlled drying stages ensure the mold develops sufficient strength and thermal resistance.

Automation improves both consistency and productivity in this stage of the process.

Dewaxing

Once the ceramic shell has hardened, the sacrificial pattern inside the mold is removed using steam autoclaves. This process leaves behind a hollow ceramic cavity corresponding to the shape of the final metal component.

Shell Firing and Mold Preparation

The ceramic shells are then fired in high-temperature furnaces to strengthen the mold and prepare it for metal casting. Additional pre-heating stages bring the mold to the correct temperature before molten metal is introduced.

Proper temperature control at this stage is critical to achieving defect-free castings.

Metal Melting and Pouring

Molten metal is produced in induction furnaces and poured into the prepared molds using controlled tilt-pour systems. These systems allow precise control over metal flow and temperature, improving casting quality.

After the metal solidifies, the ceramic shell is removed to reveal the finished metal component.

These characteristics make automotive production an effective training ground for industrial capabilities that can later support other industries.

Through the development of automotive suppliers, engineers, technicians, and manufacturing specialists gain experience in areas such as:

• robotics and automation • materials processing • advanced manufacturing techniques • supply chain coordination • industrial quality management

These capabilities are transferable to multiple sectors.

12.3 The Saudi Sovereign Manufacturing Stack

The industrial architecture proposed in this report can be described as a layered system progressing from materials to advanced industrial applications.

The structure consists of five layers:

Materials and Industrial Feedstocks

Metals, polymers, and advanced materials form the base of the manufacturing system.

Manufacturing Process Platforms

Technologies such as precision casting, stamping, machining, and robotics transform materials into industrial components.

Component and Module Manufacturing

Suppliers produce structural components, interior systems, and other modules used in larger industrial systems.

System Integration and Final Products

Vehicle manufacturers such as Vehicle Manufacturer C integrate components into finished vehicles.

Cross-Sector Industrial Expansion

Manufacturing capabilities developed within the automotive ecosystem support the emergence of additional advanced sectors.

This layered architecture enables manufacturing capabilities to evolve gradually while maintaining strong industrial integration.

12.4 Cross-Sector Industrial Expansion

One of the most important insights emerging from this analysis is that the real strategic value of automotive manufacturing lies in its ability to support broader industrial development.

The manufacturing capabilities developed through automotive production can be applied to other industries requiring similar processes and engineering expertise.

These sectors include:

• medical device manufacturing • aerospace and space systems • defense and dual-use manufacturing • desalination and water infrastructure equipment • industrial machinery and energy systems

Expanding manufacturing capabilities into these sectors improves the utilization of industrial assets and strengthens the long-term sustainability of manufacturing investments.

12.5 Strategic Role of Manufacturing Platform B

Within this architecture, the Manufacturing Platform B manufacturing platform plays a particularly important role.

While automotive supplier ecosystems focus primarily on producing components for vehicles, Manufacturing Platform B introduces manufacturing processes that can serve multiple industries.

Precision casting capabilities allow complex metal components to be produced for automotive, aerospace, medical, and industrial applications.

This cross-sector applicability makes Manufacturing Platform B an important bridge between automotive manufacturing and broader advanced industrial sectors.

12.6 Vehicle Manufacturer C as a Market Anchor

The presence of a domestic vehicle manufacturing platform is critical for sustaining the supplier ecosystem.

Vehicle Manufacturer C provides the downstream demand that supports the development of automotive suppliers within the Ecosystem Platform A ecosystem.

Without a stable vehicle manufacturing anchor, supplier localization efforts would struggle to achieve sufficient scale.

By integrating Vehicle Manufacturer C within the broader manufacturing architecture, the Kingdom can ensure that supplier development efforts are aligned with long-term industrial objectives.

12.7 Industrial Policy and Institutional Support

The development of an advanced manufacturing ecosystem requires coordination between private investors, industrial companies, and public institutions.

Institutions such as the Industrial Finance Institution D provide financing mechanisms and advisory support that can accelerate the development of manufacturing capabilities.

Industrial policy frameworks can also help ensure that supplier development, technology adoption, and workforce training initiatives remain aligned with national economic objectives.

12.8 Strategic Implementation

The roadmap presented in this report outlines a phased approach to developing the manufacturing ecosystem.

Key steps include:

• stabilizing and aligning existing industrial initiatives • expanding the automotive supplier ecosystem • establishing advanced manufacturing process platforms • integrating the manufacturing stack through industrial clustering and logistics systems • expanding manufacturing capabilities into additional sectors

Following this structured approach ensures that industrial development occurs in a coordinated and sustainable manner.

12.9 Building Long-Term Industrial Resilience

Ultimately, the goal of the proposed manufacturing architecture is to establish a resilient national industrial system.

Industrial resilience is achieved when a country possesses the ability to:

• produce critical goods domestically • maintain stable supply chains • adapt manufacturing capabilities to emerging industries • sustain technological development over time

By integrating automotive manufacturing with advanced manufacturing platforms and cross-sector industrial expansion, Saudi Arabia can build a manufacturing base capable of supporting long-term economic diversification and technological advancement.

12.10 Final Perspective

The opportunity described in this report is not simply the creation of a domestic automotive industry. It is the establishment of a national advanced manufacturing capability that can serve as a foundation for multiple strategic sectors.

Automotive manufacturing provides the initial platform, but the long-term objective is the creation of a sovereign industrial system capable of supporting industries ranging from medical technology to aerospace and energy infrastructure.

By aligning the Ecosystem Platform A ecosystem, the Manufacturing Platform B manufacturing platform, and the Vehicle Manufacturer C vehicle program within a unified industrial architecture, Saudi Arabia can take a significant step toward building such a system.

The resulting manufacturing ecosystem would not only strengthen domestic industrial capabilities but also position the Kingdom as a competitive participant in global advanced manufacturing industries.
